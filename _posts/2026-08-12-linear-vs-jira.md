Few debates in engineering teams generate more opinions than "Linear or Jira." Rather than relitigate the argument from memory, we ran the same two-week sprint — the same tickets, the same team, the same workflow — through both tools to see where each one actually earns its reputation.

## Speed and daily usability

This is where the gap is largest. Linear is fast in a way that's hard to appreciate until you're back in Jira waiting for a page to load. Keyboard shortcuts cover nearly every action, and the whole interface feels designed around not breaking a developer's flow state.

Jira, even in its modernized interface, still carries a heavier click-through cost for common actions like moving a ticket or updating a status. For teams doing dozens of small updates a day, that difference compounds.

## Where Jira still wins

Jira's customization depth is genuinely unmatched. If your organization needs highly specific workflows, complex approval chains, or integration with a broader enterprise toolchain (especially anything already running on Atlassian products), Jira accommodates that in ways Linear intentionally doesn't try to.

> "Linear feels like it was built by people who use it every day. Jira feels like it was built to be configured by someone who doesn't."

Jira's reporting and cross-project portfolio views are also considerably more mature — larger organizations tracking dozens of teams across many projects will find Jira's admin tooling more capable.

## Where Linear wins

- Issue creation and triage is dramatically faster
- Cycles (Linear's sprint equivalent) are simpler to set up and require less ongoing maintenance
- The roadmap view is genuinely useful for planning, not just a static Gantt chart
- Native integrations with GitHub/GitLab are tighter and require less configuration

For a single engineering team under roughly 50 people, Linear's opinionated simplicity is very hard to argue against once you've used it for a sprint or two.

## The honest trade-off

Jira's flexibility is also its cost — teams spend real time configuring and maintaining workflows that Linear simply doesn't ask you to think about. That's a feature if you need the flexibility, and overhead if you don't.

## Pricing, in plain terms

Both tools offer free tiers for small teams, with Linear's paid tiers scaling per-seat as team size grows, and Jira's pricing scaling similarly but with wider tier differences based on feature access (advanced permissions, portfolio views, and admin controls sit in higher tiers). Since both companies adjust tiers and included features periodically, confirm current numbers directly before budgeting for a larger rollout.

If you're a small-to-mid-size engineering team without complex compliance or portfolio-reporting needs, Linear is the easier recommendation. If you're already deep in the Atlassian ecosystem or need enterprise-grade customization, Jira remains the safer choice.
