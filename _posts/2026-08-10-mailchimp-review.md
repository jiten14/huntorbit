Mailchimp was, for a long time, the default answer to "what email tool should I use." That's less true in 2026, with a wave of newer, often cheaper competitors built specifically for creators and small businesses. We ran a small newsletter (around 3,000 subscribers) through Mailchimp for a month to see if it's still earning its place.

## Deliverability

This is the metric that matters most for an email tool, and Mailchimp performed reliably in our testing — inbox placement was consistent across the major providers we checked, with no notable spam-folder issues over the test period. That baseline reliability is still Mailchimp's strongest asset.

## The editor and templates

The drag-and-drop editor is polished and easy to use without a learning curve, and the template library covers most common newsletter layouts well. It's not the most visually distinctive tool on the market, but it's dependable, which matters more for most senders.

## Where it's fallen behind

- Pricing has become less generous at the free tier compared to several newer competitors
- Advanced automation features are increasingly gated behind higher-priced tiers
- The interface, while functional, feels dated next to newer tools built around a cleaner, more opinionated design

> "Mailchimp isn't worse than it used to be — the market around it got better and often cheaper, which changes the calculus even if the product itself held steady."

## Who it's still right for

If you're already invested in Mailchimp's ecosystem — integrations, past campaign data, existing automations — there's little reason to migrate for migration's sake. It's also a solid choice if you want an established, well-documented tool with a large support community, since that maturity counts for something when things go wrong.

## Who should look elsewhere

If you're starting from scratch with a small list and price sensitivity is a real factor, it's worth comparison shopping — several newer tools offer more generous free tiers and simpler pricing structures for exactly the use case Mailchimp originally won on.

## Pricing, in plain terms

Mailchimp's tiers scale with subscriber count and feature access (automation, advanced segmentation, and multivariate testing sit in higher tiers). Because email tool pricing shifts often and varies by list size, check Mailchimp's current pricing page against your actual subscriber count before deciding — the free tier that made Mailchimp famous is considerably more limited than it used to be.

For established senders, Mailchimp remains a safe, reliable choice. For anyone starting fresh, it's worth at least comparing against newer alternatives before committing.
